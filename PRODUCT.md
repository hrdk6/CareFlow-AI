# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

**Primary audience: interviewers and recruiters.** CareFlow AI is a portfolio project. The people it must
convince are engineering and hiring panels evaluating the author's full-stack, ML, retrieval and
security judgement — usually in a short live demo or by reading the repository.

**Product personas (the story the demo tells):** staff of an Indian hospital, each seeing a different
slice of the same data:

- **Doctor** — clinical work for patients in their department, on their care team or with an
  appointment: records, prescriptions, admissions, ML predictions, AI assistant.
- **Nurse** — clinical access to explicitly assigned patients only; writes notes and lab results.
- **Receptionist** — registration and scheduling for every patient, with no clinical data.
- **Administrator** — accounts, care-team assignments, clinician profiles, documents, audit and
  observability; reads clinical data but does not author it.

The personas are portrayed, not served: no real clinician uses the product.

## Product Purpose

A hospital information platform where every AI answer is assembled only from data the signed-in
user is allowed to see, and every claim points back to the record or document it came from.

Success means an evaluator leaves convinced of three things: the authorization model is real (the
same question returns different answers per role), the AI is grounded rather than generative
theatre, and the ML is evaluated honestly rather than oversold.

## Positioning

Most portfolio "AI healthcare" projects show a chatbot. CareFlow AI shows the controls around one:
row-level access applied inside the database before retrieval, citations validated after generation,
prompt-injection quarantine, a rules-first router that calls the LLM only when needed, and model
evaluation that includes a deliberately rejected leaky model.

## Operating Context

- Evaluated in live walkthroughs, typically switching between demo accounts to show role differences,
  and by reading the GitHub repository and docs.
- Setting is an Indian hospital: Indian patient names, `+91` phone numbers, Pune addresses, and
  English, Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, Kannada and Telugu as preferred languages.
- Runs locally (PostgreSQL + pgvector on port 5433, FastAPI, Next.js) and is intended to be hosted as
  a demo (frontend on Vercel, backend on a host with at least ~1 GB memory).
- LLM calls go to Groq (three chained models) with Google Gemini as a free-tier backup; switching is
  silent to the user.

## Capabilities and Constraints

**Capabilities (implemented):** patient registration and demographics; appointments with
availability and double-booking checks; admissions and discharges; medical records; prescriptions
with formulary, high-alert flags and allergy checks; laboratory results with reference ranges and
critical flags; clinician registration and profile maintenance; care-team management; knowledge-base
ingestion with versioning; 30-day readmission risk with SHAP explanations; length-of-stay estimate;
patient similarity; AI assistant with cited answers; audit log; AI query traces; metrics.

**Constraints:**

- All patient, clinician and document data is **synthetic**. The product is decision support
  only and **not for clinical use**; this must stay visible on every authenticated screen.
- The AI retrieves, summarises and explains; it never diagnoses or chooses treatments.
- Authorization is enforced server-side; the interface only reflects it and must never be the
  control.
- Demo accounts share one password; it must change for any public deployment.

**Terminology:** MRN (patient identifier, e.g. `P1024`), staff code (e.g. `D303`), care team,
department, route (SQL / RAG / ML / SIMILARITY / LLM), citation markers `[S#]` (document passage)
and `[R#]` (database record).

**Undecided:** production hosting provider for the backend.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **CareFlow AI**.
- Voice: plain, factual, calm. States reasons next to refusals. No hype, no exclamation marks.
- Honesty about limits is part of the brand: the product names what it cannot do.
- Light, calm interface in the original CareFlow teal (chosen 2026-09-13 after a dark monitor-style
  version read as too technical). User-facing copy describes tasks, not the access model or the engineering.

## Evidence on Hand

All figures are generated by scripts in the repository; do not restate them with different numbers.

- **Readmission model** (UCI Diabetes 130-US, patient-grouped test split, n = 15,108): ROC-AUC 0.685,
  PR-AUC 0.242 — `ml/evaluation/reports`.
- **Length of stay**: MAE 2.25 days, R² 0.078; leakage ablation reaching R² 0.413 was rejected.
- **Retrieval** (48 questions, hybrid + rerank): Recall@1 0.955, MRR 0.977, context precision 0.835,
  abstention 1.00 — `rag/evaluation/results`.
- **Patient similarity**: neighbours share department 87% of the time (random 31%).
- **Tests**: 184 backend, 13 frontend.

**Absences — never fabricate:** no real users, customers, hospitals, testimonials, deployments,
clinical validation, certifications or regulatory approval.

## Product Principles

1. **Authorization before intelligence.** Nothing reaches retrieval, models or the LLM unless the
   user may see it.
2. **Show the evidence.** Every AI answer and prediction exposes its sources, factors and limits.
3. **Honest over impressive.** Report moderate results as moderate; a rejected model is a feature.
4. **Degrade, never break.** When a model or provider fails, the product still answers from evidence
   and says so.
5. **Legible in a two-minute demo.** Role differences and safety controls should be visible without
   narration.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.2 AA**: text contrast at least 4.5:1, full keyboard operation with visible focus,
screen-reader labels on icon-only controls, no information carried by colour alone, and reduced
motion honoured.
