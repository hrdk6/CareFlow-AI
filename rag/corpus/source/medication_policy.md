# MEDICATION SAFETY AND HIGH-ALERT MEDICATIONS POLICY

Document code: MED-POL-004. Version 4. Owner: Pharmacy and Therapeutics Committee.

SYNTHETIC DEMONSTRATION DOCUMENT. Written for the CareFlow AI portfolio project; fictional policy.

## 1. Purpose

This policy reduces medication errors, with particular focus on high-alert medications, which carry a heightened risk of significant patient harm when used in error.

## 2. High-Alert Medication List

The following are designated high-alert medications at CareFlow General Hospital: all insulins (including insulin glargine and insulin lispro), anticoagulants (warfarin, apixaban, enoxaparin and unfractionated heparin), opioids (including morphine), concentrated potassium chloride, and intravenous dextrose solutions of 10% or more.

## 3. Prescribing Standards

Never use the abbreviation "U" for units; write "units" in full. Never use a trailing zero (write 5 mg, not 5.0 mg) and always use a leading zero (0.5 mg). Every prescription must include the dose, route, frequency and indication. Look-alike sound-alike (LASA) medicines are stored apart and displayed with tall-man lettering.

## 4. Independent Double Check

An independent double check by a second registered nurse or pharmacist is required before administering: any insulin infusion, any intravenous potassium, any opioid infusion or patient-controlled analgesia, and any intravenous anticoagulant.

## 5. Insulin Safety

Sliding-scale insulin used on its own is discouraged for inpatients; a basal-bolus regimen is preferred. Insulin doses are held or reduced when a patient is nil by mouth. Every hypoglycemia episode below 70 mg/dL must be reported and prompts a same-day dose review.

## 6. Anticoagulation Safety

### 6.1 Warfarin

Monitor the INR at least weekly during initiation and at least monthly once stable. The usual therapeutic range is INR 2.0 to 3.0. An INR above 4.5 is a critical value and must be reported to the prescriber immediately.

### 6.2 Apixaban

The standard dose for atrial fibrillation is 5 mg twice daily. Reduce to 2.5 mg twice daily when at least two of the following are present: age 80 years or older, body weight 60 kg or less, serum creatinine 1.5 mg/dL or higher.

### 6.3 Enoxaparin

The usual prophylactic dose is 40 mg subcutaneously once daily. When creatinine clearance is below 30 mL/min, reduce the prophylactic dose to 30 mg once daily.

## 7. Concentrated Potassium Chloride

Concentrated potassium chloride ampoules are removed from all ward stock and are available only from pharmacy. Use pre-mixed bags. The maximum infusion rate through a peripheral line is 10 mmol per hour.

## 8. Opioid Safety

Naloxone must be available wherever opioids are administered. Assess sedation and respiratory rate before each dose of an opioid.

## 9. Medication Reconciliation

Complete a medication reconciliation within 24 hours of admission and again at discharge. Verify allergies before the first dose of any new medication; a documented penicillin allergy must be checked before prescribing any beta-lactam antibiotic.

## 10. Reporting

All medication errors and near misses are reported through the incident reporting system within 24 hours. Reporting is non-punitive.
