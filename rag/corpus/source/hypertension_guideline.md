# CAREFLOW DEMO GUIDELINE: HYPERTENSION IN ADULTS

Document code: CF-GL-HTN-02. Version 2. Owner: Department of Cardiology.

SYNTHETIC DEMONSTRATION DOCUMENT. Written for the CareFlow AI portfolio project; fictional and not real clinical guidance.

## 1. Scope

This guideline covers the diagnosis and outpatient management of primary hypertension in adults aged 18 and over. Hypertension in pregnancy and in children follows separate protocols.

## 2. Blood Pressure Measurement

Measure blood pressure after the patient has been seated for 5 minutes with the arm supported at heart level, using a correctly sized cuff. Record the average of at least two readings taken one to two minutes apart. Confirm a new diagnosis with ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) or home blood pressure monitoring (HBPM) before starting long-term treatment, unless blood pressure is 180/110 mmHg or higher.

## 3. Classification

Stage 1 hypertension is a clinic blood pressure of 130 to 139 mmHg systolic or 80 to 89 mmHg diastolic. Stage 2 hypertension is 140/90 mmHg or higher. A reading of 180/120 mmHg or higher is a hypertensive crisis.

## 4. Treatment Targets

The target for most adults is below 130/80 mmHg. For adults over 80 years or with frailty, a target below 140/90 mmHg is acceptable. Patients with diabetes or chronic kidney disease should aim for below 130/80 mmHg when tolerated.

## 5. Drug Treatment

### 5.1 First-line agents

First-line options are an ACE inhibitor (for example lisinopril), an angiotensin receptor blocker (for example losartan), a dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker (for example amlodipine) or a thiazide-type diuretic (for example hydrochlorothiazide). Do not combine an ACE inhibitor with an angiotensin receptor blocker.

### 5.2 Combination therapy

Start two first-line drugs from different classes when blood pressure is more than 20/10 mmHg above target. A single-pill combination improves adherence.

### 5.3 Chronic kidney disease and albuminuria

In patients with albuminuria (UACR above 30 mg/g) an ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker is preferred and should be titrated to the maximum tolerated dose.

### 5.4 Laboratory monitoring

Check serum potassium and creatinine 1 to 2 weeks after starting or increasing an ACE inhibitor, angiotensin receptor blocker or diuretic. A creatinine rise of up to 30% is acceptable; a larger rise or potassium above 5.5 mmol/L requires dose review.

## 6. Hypertensive Urgency and Emergency

A blood pressure of 180/120 mmHg or higher WITHOUT acute target-organ damage is hypertensive urgency and can usually be managed by restarting or intensifying oral therapy with review within 24 to 72 hours. The same reading WITH acute target-organ damage (chest pain, neurological deficit, acute kidney injury, pulmonary oedema) is a hypertensive emergency and requires emergency admission through the Emergency Admission Procedure.

## 7. Lifestyle

Advise sodium intake below 2 g per day, regular physical activity of at least 150 minutes per week, weight reduction when overweight, limiting alcohol and stopping smoking.

## 8. Follow-up

Review monthly after any treatment change until the target is reached, then every 3 to 6 months.
